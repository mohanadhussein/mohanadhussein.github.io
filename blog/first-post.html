The current view of the bioinformatics job market

2026-11-09

This is the first post in this blog, where I try to lay down multiple point of views in a certain topic relevant in a way or another to bioinformatics. The aim is not to grow an audience or self branding etc, but rather to present my point of view that mihgt be uncommon. My thought process is mostly based on comparisons and pattern recognition supported by some effort to eliminate noise in the pursuit of fullfilling career and life choices.

From several conversations with PhDs in prestigous research institutions and industry leaders both in the EU, I notice a shared pattern of fear towards the future of AI excellence, rightfully though. But what I often observe to be missing, is the consideration of how new technology evolves and that the fast pace in development doesn't necessarily mean that the majority of bioinformaticians will loose their jobs in the near future. First because AI makes mistakes, no matter how good LLMs become, almost in every domain you can find examples for when/where the human experience and knowledge is irreplaceable. Even if a big portion of routine tasks is handed over to AI tools, human-in-the-loop systems are still prioritized and will likely not be abondened for fully automated decision processes, mainly in the medical domain as one might guess. Secondly, because there is still considerable gap between the ability of our societies to operate in a world were AI tools (including humanoids) are essential element in every day life; Qouting Mo Gawdat, the former head of Google X: "What AI can not achieve is the genuine human interaction". Here, I do not refer to lights-out factories and warehouses but to the complete reliance on AI tools in our everyday-life. TBC
